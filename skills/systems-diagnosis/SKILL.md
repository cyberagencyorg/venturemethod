# Systems Diagnosis

**Purpose:** Analyze interacting causes, constraints, incentives, feedback loops, dependencies and power within a defined system.

## When to use

A stubborn operational, market, institutional or societal problem requires a systems-level diagnosis instead of single-factor attribution.

## Inputs

System boundary, timescale, actors, observed behaviors, incentives, policies, feedback, data sources, competing causal explanations.

## Method

1. Define system boundary and chosen level of analysis; mark what is out of scope.
2. Distinguish observed relationships, causal hypotheses, moral judgments and conjectures; provide explicit disconfirming routes.
3. Map actors, resources, authorities, bottlenecks, flows, dependencies, reinforcing and balancing loops.
4. Study delays, second-order effects, unintended consequences, failure modes and path dependence.
5. Generate intervention alternatives at several leverage points with likely winners, losers and ethical constraints.
6. Choose measurable low-cost probes; validate causal premises before claiming intervention effectiveness.

## Outputs

System boundary; actor and dependency map; causal hypothesis register; leverage options; risk/ethics review; experimental plan.

## Quality gates

- Correlation, intentionality and causation remain distinct.
- Do not reduce complex real-world causes to one actor without evidence.
- Make power and stakeholder interests discussable without demonizing groups.
- Specify observable measures and failure conditions.
- Prefer evidence-linked, economical work proportionate to actual decision risk.

## Handoff contract

State engagement ID, bounded objective, source and claim IDs, open assumptions, options, reviewer, approval owner, and next test.

## Evaluation

Use `evals/systems-diagnosis.json` with representative, ambiguous, and adversarial cases against a no-skill baseline. Structural checks alone are not effectiveness proof.

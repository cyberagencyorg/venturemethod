# Business Model

**Purpose:** Design and stress-test how an offering creates, delivers and captures value, including cash mechanics and operating constraints.

## When to use

Developing a venture, advisory offer, services-to-product transition, pricing or commercial redesign.

## Inputs

Segments, offers, jobs, substitutes, acquisition channels, unit assumptions, costs, revenue, delivery capacity.

## Method

1. Map each business model separately, not multiple unrelated segments into one canvas.
2. Identify value proposition, channels, retention drivers, value chain, suppliers, activities and dependencies.
3. Build explicit unit-economics assumptions with input source, confidence and sensitivity.
4. Test pricing and monetization against willingness to pay, delivery economics and switching costs.
5. Surface cash conversion, working capital, gross margin versus contribution margin and break-even thresholds.

## Outputs

Business-model options, assumptions table, economics worksheet specification, pricing tests and recommended model.

## Quality gates

- No invented financial performance.
- Distinguish gross bookings, revenue, gross profit and cash flow.
- State third-party framework attribution when used.
- Label observations, sourced third-party assertions, analysis and recommendations separately.
- If required information is absent, disclose it and propose next checks; never fabricate completion.
- Prefer the shortest defensible workflow appropriate to the stakes.

## Handoff contract

Include engagement ID, objective, references to source IDs and claim IDs, unresolved issues, recommended next action, and approval owner.

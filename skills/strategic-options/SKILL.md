# Strategic Options

**Purpose:** Generate and compare genuinely distinct courses of action across feasibility, economics, agency, time and consequences.

## When to use

A consequential decision has more than one plausible solution or has been prematurely narrowed.

## Inputs

Objective, constraints, current state, stakeholders, evidence, capabilities, resources and uncertainty.

## Method

1. Define desired outcome and the system boundary; characterize inertia and baseline path.
2. Generate at least three meaningful alternatives: status quo, incremental change and a nontrivial strategic option.
3. Evaluate tradeoffs, reversibility, dependency chain, ethics, timing and second-order effects.
4. Compare against common criteria without hiding unquantified factors in a weighted total.
5. Recommend a bounded next bet and review trigger, not false certainty.

## Outputs

Strategic options memo; tradeoff matrix; decision rationale; assumptions and reversible next steps.

## Quality gates

- Do not present preferences as measured impact.
- Include a defensible alternative and strongest counterargument.
- Escalate decisions that affect third-party rights or require authorization.
- Label observations, sourced third-party assertions, analysis and recommendations separately.
- If required information is absent, disclose it and propose next checks; never fabricate completion.
- Prefer the shortest defensible workflow appropriate to the stakes.

## Handoff contract

Include engagement ID, objective, references to source IDs and claim IDs, unresolved issues, recommended next action, and approval owner.

## Evaluation

Use `evals/strategic-options.json` to test relevance, factual discipline and output usefulness against a no-skill baseline.

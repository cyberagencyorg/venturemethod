# Intake Scoping

**Purpose:** Turn an ambiguous client, venture, industry, or deliverable request into an explicit decision-oriented engagement contract.

## When to use

A new problem, request, brief, RFP, client or idea arrives; do not activate for routine clerical edits.

## Inputs

Original request verbatim; known actors; intended beneficiary; decision maker; deadlines; commercial constraints; prior context and files.

## Method

1. Identify the literal request separately from the underlying problem and why now.
2. State the object of analysis: company, market, product, capability, transaction, or venture; distinguish client identity from project identity.
3. Specify who can authorize actions, what success would change, what is out of scope, constraints, deadlines, and confidentiality.
4. Identify unknowns that actually affect the next decision; answer from existing records first rather than re-asking.
5. Choose quick, standard or deep depth; propose useful artifacts, not an indiscriminate checklist.

## Outputs

Engagement charter with objective, stakes, decision, boundaries, assumptions, named unknowns, depth, authorization, and first useful deliverable.

## Quality gates

- No invented stakeholder mandate or timeline.
- Client words and practitioner interpretation remain distinguishable.
- No commitment or external contact without authorization.
- Label observations, sourced third-party assertions, analysis and recommendations separately.
- If required information is absent, disclose it and propose next checks; never fabricate completion.
- Prefer the shortest defensible workflow appropriate to the stakes.

## Handoff contract

Include engagement ID, objective, references to source IDs and claim IDs, unresolved issues, recommended next action, and approval owner.

## Evaluation

Use `evals/intake-scoping.json` to test relevance, factual discipline and output usefulness against a no-skill baseline.

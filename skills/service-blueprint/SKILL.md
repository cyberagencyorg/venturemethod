# Service Blueprint

**Purpose:** Investigate delivery systems across user experience, staff, processes, data, vendors and operational constraints.

## When to use

A service, business process or operating model needs redesign, automation, customer experience improvement or cost reduction.

## Inputs

Service boundaries, customer journey, staff workflows, systems, process data, exception cases, time/cost, security and controls.

## Method

1. Observe the existing end-to-end journey and handoffs before proposing automation.
2. Map visible touchpoints, backstage work, supporting systems, ownership, queues, failure points, exceptions and controls.
3. Quantify rework, wait, error, and customer impact only with observed data or clearly labeled estimates.
4. Identify root causes, upstream incentives and dependency constraints; compare nontechnical fixes to automation options.
5. Define future-state service blueprint, capability gap, responsible owners and transition risks.
6. Pilot on representative cases and measure customer outcome, process quality, actual workload and unintended effects.

## Outputs

Current/future service blueprint; workflow and ownership map; pain and constraint register; intervention options; pilot criteria.

## Quality gates

- Include exceptions and frontline accounts, not only leadership narratives.
- Do not confuse diagram completion with operational validation.
- Protect employee and client data; human review of personnel impacts.
- Avoid automating broken processes without root-cause work.
- Prefer evidence-linked, economical work proportionate to actual decision risk.

## Handoff contract

State engagement ID, bounded objective, source and claim IDs, open assumptions, options, reviewer, approval owner, and next test.

## Evaluation

Use `evals/service-blueprint.json` with representative, ambiguous, and adversarial cases against a no-skill baseline. Structural checks alone are not effectiveness proof.

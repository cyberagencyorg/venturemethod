# Experiment Design

**Purpose:** Choose inexpensive, ethical, falsifiable tests that distinguish competing hypotheses before major resource commitments.

## When to use

Uncertainty prevents a product, strategy, venture, positioning or operating decision.

## Inputs

Testable hypothesis, baseline, target group, constraints, impact of failure, available data.

## Method

1. Name the exact risky assumption and rival explanations.
2. Select method: interview, concierge, pretotype, landing page, prototype, pricing test, controlled trial or field study.
3. Define sample/selection bias, measurable success/failure rule, stopping condition and evidence collection.
4. Decide who may be affected, permissions, privacy protections and what users will actually experience.
5. Prespecify how each possible result changes the decision; capture outcome and learning.

## Outputs

Experiment card with hypothesis, setup, measurement, sample, pass/fail threshold, ethics, cost and decision branch.

## Quality gates

- Do not report imagined experiment outcomes.
- No dark patterns, deceptive pricing or unapproved customer outreach.
- Do not turn weak sample evidence into universal proof.
- Label observations, sourced third-party assertions, analysis and recommendations separately.
- If required information is absent, disclose it and propose next checks; never fabricate completion.
- Prefer the shortest defensible workflow appropriate to the stakes.

## Handoff contract

Include engagement ID, objective, references to source IDs and claim IDs, unresolved issues, recommended next action, and approval owner.

## Evaluation

Use `evals/experiment-design.json` to test relevance, factual discipline and output usefulness against a no-skill baseline.

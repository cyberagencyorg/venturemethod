# Competitive Positioning

**Purpose:** Resolve buyer-relevant differentiation and category context before anyone writes a tagline, homepage hero or agency description.

## When to use
Use for positioning, market-category selection, product/service differentiation, best-fit audience and market messaging whose value has not yet been established.

## Inputs
Project marketing context, buyer jobs, alternatives actually considered, verified capability, commercial evidence and existing decisions.

## Prerequisites
Read the canonical project marketing context and accepted company identity. If neither exists, invoke `marketing-context` narrowly. Reuse `brand-architecture` for parent/subbrand questions.

## Method
1. **Buying occasion:** define the specific buyer, trigger, job and the choice they face.
2. **Real alternatives:** document what the buyer actually would do without us: incumbents, internal team, manual work, hiring, consultants, postponement. Separate witnessed alternatives from hypothetical competitors.
3. **Comparative capabilities:** list only genuine capabilities and evidence of how they differ against those alternatives; no self-congratulatory adjectives masquerading as differentiation.
4. **Value:** for each comparative capability, explain the practical buyer outcome and why the alternative cannot match it under the same constraints. Identify which differences are material.
5. **Best-fit buyers:** identify the characteristics of buyers who disproportionately value those differences, the purchasing committee and exclusions.
6. **Category last:** evaluate familiar category frames against buyer expectations, with tradeoffs for clarity, discoverability and misclassification. Creating a category has a large education cost; do so deliberately.
7. **Test alternatives:** produce 2 or 3 positioning routes, their rivals and a focused reality check: actual client language, sales notes, buyer interview or decision test.
8. **Record decision:** save rationale, rejected route, evidence strength and revisiting conditions to marketing context; do not turn a proposed descriptor into an approved fact.

## Outputs
Competitive alternatives table, attribute-to-value map, best-fit buyer description, market frame options and recommended hypothesis with evidence gaps.

## Quality gates
- Never start from a clever headline.
- No market category selected before alternatives, differentiated capability, value and best-fit buyer.
- Company purpose, brand philosophy, product differentiation and website messaging are distinct artifacts.
- Evidence of competitive superiority cannot be invented.
- If alternatives are unknown, return a provisional positioning with explicit buyer research needed.

## Composition
`marketing-context` -> `competitive-positioning` -> `brand-architecture` (when multi-brand) -> `marketing-copywriting`. Do not run the full chain for a trivial sentence edit.
